### Land disputes, expropriation, and investor-state arbitration

**Legal protection:** The US-Senegal BIT guarantees against expropriation/nationalization except for public interest, per law, with "fair and prior compensation" ([State Dept 2025 Investment Climate Statement](https://www.state.gov/reports/2025-investment-climate-statements/senegal) — direct fetch was blocked (403); sourced via search summary, verify against the [primary PDF](https://www.state.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/638719_2025-Senegal-Investment-Climate-Statement.pdf)).

## 2. Currency & Capital Controls — COMPLETE

### The XOF peg and guarantee mechanism
Senegal's currency (XOF) is pegged to the euro at 1 EUR = 655.957 XOF since 1999. The French Treasury provides an unconditional, unlimited convertibility guarantee via an overdraft facility to BCEAO — a central-bank-to-central-bank backstop, not a right of any individual investor to convert unlimited XOF on demand ([Banque de France](https://www.banque-france.fr/en/banque-de-france/africa-france-partnerships); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CFA_franc)). The historical 50%-of-reserves deposit requirement at the French Treasury was dropped in 2019/effective 2021 (the "Eco" reform); the peg and guarantee were retained.

**Translation note:** 西非法郎 (XOF, issued by BCEAO) must be kept distinct from 中非法郎 (XAF, CEMAC zone) in the Chinese materials — they are separate, non-interchangeable currencies despite the shared "CFA franc" name.

### Repatriation of profits/dividends
The **new 2025 Investment Code** (Loi n°2025-16 du 27 septembre 2025, replacing the 2004 code) guarantees "freedom of capital and benefits transfer" and currency convertibility for foreign investors — **but expressly subject to UEMOA/WAEMU regulations** ([Ecofin Agency](https://www.ecofinagency.com/news-finances/2209-48884-senegal-adopts-new-investment-code-to-boost-economic-attractiveness); [CNES primary text](https://cnes.sn/wp-content/uploads/2025/10/Loi-n%C2%B0-2025-16-portant-Code-des-Investissements.pdf)). Repatriation is **legally guaranteed in principle but procedural, not instantaneous**:
- A statistical "déclaration d'investissements étrangers" must be filed with BCEAO/the Directorate of External Finance under (now) **Règlement n°06/2024/CM/UEMOA** — this is a registration obligation, not a prior-approval gate.
- Investment flows and dividend/interest/liquidation proceeds must be routed through an **approved local bank (intermédiaire agréé)**.
- A **new BCEAO instruction effective 1 August 2025** tightened the obligation to surrender/sell foreign currency to BCEAO on non-resident investment proceeds and resident dividends received abroad — a **tightening**, not a loosening, of FX centralization, and the single most important recent data point for structuring this deal (source blocked 403 on direct fetch — verify with UEMOA-qualified counsel before finalizing).

**Net assessment:** peg/convertibility-into-EUR risk is low (decades-long, never-triggered French guarantee); **administrative/liquidity friction in actually moving profits out is the real risk** given the 2024-2025 tightening.

### CFA-exit / currency-reform political risk
PASTEF campaigned in 2024 on eventual CFA replacement ("Eco" or national currency), reflecting Sahel-wide monetary-sovereignty sentiment ([Al Jazeera](https://www.aljazeera.com/features/2024/2/23/burkina-faso-mali-and-niger-debate-exiting-cfa-zone)). By 2025 this rhetoric had **visibly softened in practice**: reporting says "no longer any talk of renouncing the CFA franc" near-term, and Senegal actually **increased XOF-denominated bond issuance** on the regional UMOA-Titres market in early 2025, becoming the leading issuer — behavior inconsistent with an imminent break ([Capmad](https://www.capmad.com/economy-en/exit-from-the-cfa-franc-the-senegalese-president-presents-his-options/)). **Assessment: real but low-to-medium-probability medium-term risk, not an imminent dealbreaker** — disclose candidly, note the 2025 bond-market behavior as a countervailing signal, and suggest EUR/USD-denominated financing tranches as a mitigant.

## 3. Taxation — COMPLETE

- **Corporate income tax:** 30% (raised from 25%) under the Code Général des Impôts ([Sénégal Services](https://senegalservices.sn/actualite/limpot-sur-les-societes-au-senegal-passe-de-25-a-30)). A minimum flat tax (~0.5% of prior-year revenue, floor/ceiling unverified) applies when loss-making.
- **VAT:** single standard rate of **18%** ([Grant Thornton](https://www.grantthornton.global/en/insights/indirect-tax-guide/indirect-tax---Senegal/)). Separate recurring property tax (contribution foncière) reported at ~5% of value for non-industrial property, ~7.5% for industrial — keep 房产税 distinct from 增值税(TVA) in Chinese materials.
- **Investment Code incentives (2025 Code):** minimum investment threshold for eligibility lowered from 100M to **15M XOF**, dematerialized one-stop shop with 10-working-day processing, territorialized incentives, local-content requirement, fiscal/customs stability guarantees for 3-5 years. During construction: VAT suspension + customs-duty exemption on approved project equipment; during operation: investment tax credits against corporate tax. **All incentives require an APIX/one-stop-shop approval (agrément) — not automatic.**
- **Solar-specific incentive:** a 2020 decree exempts 22 categories of renewable-energy equipment (panels, inverters, batteries, controllers, etc.) from VAT; local manufacturers of renewable-energy goods may deduct 30% of taxable profit (decree number/current validity should be reconfirmed).
- **Affordable-housing incentive:** reduced registration duties/tax rates reportedly exist for capped-price housing (a cited "5%/1%" figure could not be traced to a primary source — treat as unconfirmed/indicative only).
- **China-Senegal DTA:** China and Senegal **signed** a bilateral tax agreement in Beijing in October 2023 (BRI tax-cooperation framing) ([China Daily](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202310/17/WS652e531ea31090682a5e9098.html)), but **entry-into-force could not be confirmed** — Senegal did not appear in China's 2024-2025 batches of newly-effective treaties. **Treat the DTA as signed-but-not-yet-confirmed-in-force; do not represent it to investors as currently operative.** Absent an active DTA, Senegal applies ~10% withholding on dividends to non-residents and ~20% on services absent treaty relief (unverified against primary DGID text), and Chinese investors would rely on China's unilateral foreign tax credit rules instead of treaty relief — flag for tax counsel, consider whether an intermediate treaty-jurisdiction holding structure is worth evaluating.

## 4. Legal Framework & Land Tenure — COMPLETE

### Investment Code (2025)
Senegal adopted a **new Investment Code** on 19 September 2025 (Loi n°2025-16, in force 27 Sept 2025), replacing the 2004 code ([Ecofin](https://www.ecofinagency.com/news-finances/2209-48884-senegal-adopts-new-investment-code-to-boost-economic-attractiveness); [DLA Piper Africa](https://www.dlapiperafrica.com/en/senegal/insights/2025/new_investment-code)). Key features: digitalized one-stop shop (10-working-day target), VAT suspension + customs exemption on approved-project equipment during construction, income-tax relief phased out over the last 3 years of the benefit period, **stability guarantee 3 years (Dakar/Thiès) or 5 years (other regions)** — i.e. the Code rewards siting outside the capital, new "Strategic Investment" and "Socially Responsible Investment" regimes, and reinforced repatriation/convertibility guarantees. **Eligibility thresholds** under the old 2004 code were CFA 100M (~$165K) minimum investment + 150 Senegalese jobs created — **whether these carry over unchanged to the 2025 Code is unconfirmed**, verify with counsel.

Administering body: **APIX** (Agence de Promotion des Investissements et des Grands Travaux), now under the Ministry of Investment Promotion — runs the Guichet Unique for company registration, investment-code approval (agrément), and land-search assistance; jointly with CTSI handles investor grievances against public administrations.

### Special Economic Zones
Governed by Loi n°2017-06/07, Décret 2017-1174. Senegal now has **4 SEZs**, including the Diamniadio-area zone (ZESID, successor to the original "DISEZ"). Zone A enterprises get a **15% corporate tax rate** (vs. standard 30%) + duty-free import/export + benefits up to 25 years + unrestricted FX transactions in-zone. **Whether residential real estate or standalone solar generation qualify as eligible activities in any specific zone is unconfirmed** — check directly with APIX/the zone Administrator; note that renewable-energy VAT exemption (below) is available **outside** the SEZ regime too, so SEZ status may not even be necessary for the PV component.

### Renewable-energy incentives (general tax code, not SEZ-dependent)
VAT exemption on renewable-energy production/self-consumption/R&D equipment under Arrêté n°010158 du 28 mai 2020; further tax reduction under Art. 253-bis of the General Tax Code. Sector framework: Loi n°2021-31 (Electricity Code) + Loi n°2021-32 (creates CRSE regulator); Arts. 25/57 give renewables priority grid dispatch. Government target: 40% renewable share by 2030; solar was 47.4% of the renewables mix in 2023.

### Foreign ownership of real estate
**No blanket nationality bar** — a foreigner can hold titre foncier in their own name via the same notarial/registration process as a Senegalese national. The real constraint is **land category, not buyer nationality** (see below). Reported (unconfirmed) frictions: a possible non-resident "purchase authorization," and Ministry of Economy/Finance authorization for private sales over CFA 10 million — both should be checked with local counsel and built into the transaction timeline given a large development will exceed that threshold.

### Land tenure — the actual risk driver
Three tenure categories, ascending security:
1. **Domaine national** (Loi n°64-46, 1964) — commonly cited (unverified precisely) as covering the large majority of Senegal's land. **Legally inalienable** — cannot be sold/leased directly to an individual; the most a foreign-backed company can get directly is a revocable **droit d'usage** via local council **délibération**. **Highest risk category** — precarious, historically excluded outsiders, frequent informal multiple-sales fraud.
2. **Domaine de l'État** (State's private domain, after reclassification out of domaine national) — enables a **bail emphytéotique** (long-term real property right, mortgageable/transferable/sub-leasable), Senegalese practice reportedly ~30 years renewable +20 (some sources cite up to 50-year caps — confirm in the actual lease contract). Carries a **development covenant** (commonly ~24 months) — miss it and the lease can be revoked. **Medium risk** — real, bankable right, but not full ownership.
3. **Titre foncier** — full freehold, "inattaquable et définitif," enforceable against all. **Lowest risk once obtained**, but even titled land carries practical fraud/multiple-sale-history risk — a full title-history search (état hypothécaire) via notary is essential regardless.

**Realistic path for this project:** (1) secure a bail emphytéotique on State-domain land (with APIX facilitation) → (2) execute development per the lease covenant → (3) apply to convert to titre foncier once the minimum holding period (commonly cited ~5 years) and development conditions are met. Confirm which tenure category the actual site currently sits in before any capital commitment — this single fact drives most of the land-risk profile.

### Company incorporation
OHADA Uniform Act on Commercial Companies applies. **SA** (public limited company, min. capital CFA 10M, ¼ paid in, board required) is the more likely vehicle for a multi-investor project than SARL (minimum-capital figure disputed across sources — CFA 100,000 vs. OHADA default CFA 1,000,000, confirm with counsel). **Full foreign ownership permitted, no local-partner requirement.** Registration timeline reported anywhere from 48 hours to 3 weeks depending on source — present a conservative 1-3 week range to investors.

### China-Senegal BIT status
**Could not be confirmed to exist.** China has BITs with 29+ Sub-Saharan African states (Ghana 1989 was the first) but Senegal was not confirmed on that list. Absent a BIT, protection would rely on the Investment Code's own guarantees, ICSID access on a contractual (not treaty) basis, or a holding structure routed through a jurisdiction that does have a Senegal BIT (e.g., the US does, since 1990) — flag for investor counsel, do not assert BIT coverage in investor materials.

## 6-7. Dispute Resolution & Sector-Specific Compliance — COMPLETE

### OHADA / CCJA
Senegal joined OHADA in 1995 (17 member states, mostly Francophone West/Central Africa). OHADA Uniform Acts are **directly applicable, superseding conflicting national law** without domestic transposition — covers commercial companies, arbitration, secured transactions, and insolvency, all relevant to the JV vehicle and its contracting/security arrangements. The 2017-revised **Uniform Act on Arbitration** added "arbitration without privity" (investor-state arbitration via standing consent in national law/a BIT) and a strict **15-day deadline** for domestic courts to recognize/enforce an award.

The **CCJA** (Common Court of Justice and Arbitration, Abidjan) has a dual role unfamiliar to PRC counsel: (1) supranational supreme court with final appellate jurisdiction over OHADA-law disputes from all 17 member states' national courts, and (2) a standing arbitral institution under its own Rules (modeled on 1998 ICC Rules) — explain this dual nature explicitly in the Chinese-language materials rather than assuming it translates.

### ICSID & New York Convention
Senegal is an ICSID Contracting State (commonly cited since 21 May 1967, unverified against the primary list) with an active case history (SOABI v. Senegal — a landmark enforcement/sovereign-immunity case after France's Cour de Cassation grappled with executing the award against state assets; Menzies v. Senegal; the pending 2025 Woodside Energy case). Also a New York Convention party (accession reported 17 Oct 1994, in force 15 Jan 1995, unverified against UNCITRAL's primary table). **Practical takeaway:** formal enforcement machinery exists and works, but even "final" awards can face real friction — sovereign-immunity questions, court-ordered suspension pending seat-side annulment proceedings, mandatory certified French translation — budget for this rather than assuming instant enforceability.

**Recommended clause structure** (general OHADA-investor market pattern, not a specific named recommendation): CCJA arbitration seated in the OHADA zone for ordinary commercial/JV disputes (fast, supranational enforceability); ICC arbitration seated in Paris as an alternative for higher-value/cross-border finance disputes; ICSID/BIT arbitration reserved for sovereign-conduct claims (expropriation, denial of justice) if a treaty basis exists — separate from ordinary commercial disagreements.

### Construction permitting
Ministry of Urban Renewal, Housing & Living Environment (via DGUA/DGCH/ANAT) administers the **permis de construire**, legal basis Art. 68 of the Code de l'Urbanisme + Décret n°2010-99 (Building Code). Required: application to the mayor, land title proof, cadastral plan, 5 sets of 1:100 architectural plans. Digitalized **TeleDAC** platform targets 28 days (simple)/40 days (complex) processing, though practitioner sources say full review "can take up to 3 months" — present as targeted, not guaranteed, timelines. Unauthorized construction can draw fines up to CFA 10M (unverified against primary statute).

### Environmental Impact Assessment
Governed by the Code de l'Environnement — originally Loi n°2001-01, reportedly **superseded by Loi n°2023-15 (2 Aug 2023)**, which restructures project risk categories — **confirm which version's thresholds currently apply before assuming anything from the 2001-era secondary sources**. DEEC (Direction de l'Environnement et des Établissements Classés) administers; distinguishes a simplified vs. in-depth EIES; process historically ~3 months including a DEEC-organized public hearing within 1 month of submission. **No specific numeric threshold for triggering EIES on a residential project was confirmed** — verify directly with DEEC. The Bokhol solar plant's AfDB-supported EIES is a usable precedent template for the PV component.

### Labor law
Code du Travail is **under active reform** (draft expected/prioritized 2025-2026) — treat current secondary-source descriptions as provisional. Expatriate workers need a **Permis de Travail** (Ministry of Labour, ~1 year, renewable); expatriate contracts must be fixed-term and pre-approved by the Director General of Labor (void if refused). Employers must provide housing/housing allowance to expatriate staff. **The confirmed 51% local-hiring quota applies only to oil/gas, maritime, and mining** — no confirmed construction-sector quota was found, though stricter enforcement/labor-market-testing trends should be expected. ECOWAS nationals are exempt from the work-permit requirement.

### Solar-specific regulatory notes
Electricity Code Arts. 57-58 reportedly grant priority grid connection to licensed renewable producers (verify article numbers against the current consolidated text). SENELEC is the interconnection counterparty; sector regulator CRSE sets feed-in ceiling rates under a hybrid "net metering + feed-in tariff" policy pitched below retail rates (specialist/advocacy source — verify with CRSE directly before using in financial modeling). Renewable capacity more than doubled 2018-2021 (218MW→460MW, 226MW of it solar). **No specific SENELEC tariff schedule, net-metering cap, or interconnection timeline for a captive residential-development PV system was found — this is a concrete open item to raise directly with SENELEC/CRSE or Senegalese energy counsel before finalizing PV economics.**
