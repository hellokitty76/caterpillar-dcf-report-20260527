# Senegal Solar PV Research Brief — Multiple Scenarios for a Chinese-Backed Residential Development

*Compiled July 2026. All figures cited; where no reliable source exists, a reasoned range is given and flagged as an estimate.*

## 1. Senegal's Electricity/Solar Regulatory Landscape

- **SENELEC** (national utility): vertically integrated, retains transmission/distribution monopoly; generation liberalized — IPPs supply ~70% of generation. Solar/wind IPPs were 21% of total annual generation in 2022 ([IEA](https://www.iea.org/reports/senegal-case-study/utility-scale-solar-pv-and-wind-in-senegal-overcoming-regional-related-risk-perceptions-with-an-attractive-investment-proposition)).
- **CRSE** (regulator, under the 2021 Electricity Code, Loi n°2021-31) oversees IPPs, rural electrification, renewables, energy efficiency.
- **ASER** = rural electrification agency (not the relevant regulator for an urban/peri-urban development). **ANER** = cross-sector renewables promotion agency, a stakeholder in self-consumption projects alongside the Ministry of Energy, CRSE, SENELEC.
- **Self-production/net-billing regime — Decree 2023-286 (7 Feb 2023), the key regulatory fact for sizing decisions:**
  - **Declaration regime** (simple notification, no license): **solar PV ≤ 1 MW installed capacity** per installation.
  - **License regime** (CRSE-vetted, 25-year term, Ministry of Energy issuance): required above that threshold, or for *any* installation selling surplus to the grid regardless of size.
  - Surplus-sale rate fixed by CRSE; self-producer capacity capped at 120% of peak demand (residential) / 110% of rated power (professional); self-producer bears connection costs.
  - **Practical read: per-building rooftop under ~1MWp can likely use the light declaration regime; a centralized development-wide solar farm will almost certainly need the full 25-year CRSE license — verify against the primary Journal Officiel text before finalizing (only secondary/law-firm summaries were accessible).**
- **Targets/precedent:** PSE (Plan Sénégal Émergent) + JETP (June 2023, up to €2.5bn mobilized) target **40% renewable installed capacity by 2030**; already ~29.1% of ~1,903.8MW installed capacity is renewable. IFC Scaling Solar delivered Ten Merina (~30MW), Bokhol (20MW), and round 2's Kael+Kahone (~44MWp combined, Meridiam/Engie). **Malicounda (22MW, Thiès) is 90%-owned by CGN Europe Energy — the European renewables arm of China General Nuclear Power Corporation (CGN), a Chinese SOE — the strongest direct Chinese-Senegal solar precedent (see §4).**

## 2. Solar Resource / Feasibility

- **GHI:** ~1,825-2,233 kWh/m²/yr (~5.0-6.1 kWh/m²/day) nationally; highest in the north/interior (Tambacounda ~2,188, northern belt ~2,179-2,233 kWh/m²/yr); Dakar/coastal slightly lower (~5.43 kWh/m²/day) due to humidity/aerosols ([Global Solar Atlas](https://globalsolaratlas.info/download/senegal)).
- **Seasonality:** peaks in dry season (~Nov-May), dips in rainy season/hivernage (Jun-Oct) from cloud cover.
- **Real-world performance:** Ten Merina — PR 74.3%, final yield 4.61 kWh/kWp/day (≈1,680 kWh/kWp/yr, ~19% capacity factor). Bokhol — CF ~16% in best months. **Planning range for the proposal: ~1,500-1,800 kWh/kWp/year (CF ~17-21%) for fixed-tilt**; single-axis tracking pushes higher at added cost/cleaning complexity.
- **Heat derating:** standard c-Si temperature coefficient ~0.3-0.45%/°C above 25°C cell temp; no Senegal-specific coefficient study found (engineering-phase gap).
- **Dust/Harmattan soiling — first-order design risk:** West Africa studies found soiling losses can **exceed 50%** in worst-affected locations (26-53% recorded in Niamey); heavy Harmattan soiling can cut residential output up to 30% in just two weeks without cleaning. Recommended peak-Harmattan (Dec-Feb) cleaning cadence: **~every 2 weeks**. SSA-wide PV-cleaning cost could reach ~$1bn/yr by 2030 at scale. **This favors centralized, easy-to-service arrays (scenario b/c below) over widely dispersed rooftop installations, and should be budgeted explicitly into O&M for any scenario.**

## 3. Multiple PV Scenarios — Comparison Table

*CAPEX figures are benchmark ranges from Africa/global cost literature, not site-specific EPC quotes — treat as planning-level.*

Cost anchors: global utility-scale PV fell to ~$758/kW (2023, IRENA); **Sub-Saharan Africa averages ~$1,093/kW** (second-highest region globally, driven by thin supply chains/import costs/financing premiums) → **use $1,000-1,300/kW as the realistic Africa ground-mount benchmark**. Battery storage: global 2024 core-equipment cost ~$165-192/kWh (China turnkey ~$73/kWh, Europe ~$177/kWh, US ~$219/kWh); no SSA-specific BESS figure found — **reasoned estimate $250-450/kWh installed in West Africa**, flagged as estimate.

| Scenario | Est. CAPEX/kW | Ownership / role split | Key risk | Best fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **(a) Rooftop distributed PV per building** (grid-tied, net-billing/self-consumption) | ~$1,000-1,400/kW (Africa C&I/residential premium, *estimate*) | Individual homeowners/HOA own arrays; Chinese partner = equipment supplier + installer only | Fragmented O&M makes biweekly Harmattan cleaning hard/costly per building; **no Senegal-specific statute on shared/condominium rooftop-PV rights was found** — a real legal gap (see §6); each unit likely sits under the ≤1MW declaration regime individually, but aggregated development-wide footprint could draw scrutiny | Villas/low-rise units where individual owner bill-savings and buy-in matter more than utility-style revenue |
| **(b) Centralized ground-mount solar farm + private microgrid** for the whole development | ~$1,000-1,300/kW (in line with Ten Merina/Bokhol/Malicounda-class projects) | Development/SPV owns & operates; Chinese partner as EPC + possible minority equity (mirrors CGN's 90% stake model at Malicounda) | Very likely **exceeds 1MW → triggers the 25-year CRSE license regime**; land-take competes with saleable housing/amenity land | Master-planned developments with dedicated utility land and appetite for a "campus utility" model — centralized, easier to clean |
| **(c) Hybrid PV + battery storage** (± diesel genset backup) | PV as above + battery ~$250-450/kWh (*estimate*) | Development/SPV owns PV+BESS; Chinese partner supplies integrated PV+inverter+BESS package (LFP ~85% of global battery shipments); genset (if kept) run by facilities operator | Battery capex raises payback period; genset reintroduces fuel-price/import risk; ~10-15yr LFP replacement cycle must be budgeted | **Best matched to Senegal's actual grid-reliability profile** — IEA explicitly flags transmission bottlenecks/stability constraints as an investor risk. Recommended default for a reliability-marketed premium product |
| **(d) PV + Chinese EPC/O&M service model — PPA or BOOT/BOO** | Same hardware CAPEX as (b)/(c), borne by the Chinese EPC/investor, recovered via a 15-25yr PPA tariff (regional African PPA tenors typically 20-25yrs per World Bank PPP guidance) | Chinese EPC/investor designs/finances/builds/owns/operates for the PPA/BOOT term, then transfers (BOOT) or continues as long-term IPP (BOO); development pays per-kWh, no upfront capex | Requires the development itself to be a creditworthy long-term offtaker — a much thinner counterparty than SENELEC, raising financing cost; **currency mismatch if tariff is USD/EUR-indexed against XOF-denominated HOA fee income** (see §6) | Best if the Chinese side wants a long-term revenue stream (not a one-off equipment sale) and the development can offer credit enhancement (escrow, DFI guarantee, corporate parent guarantee) to make the PPA bankable |

**Chinese partner's likely role across scenarios:** major module/inverter suppliers (JinkoSolar, LONGi, Trina, JA Solar; Huawei/Sungrow/Growatt inverters; CATL/BYD-class BESS) would supply hardware via EU/Africa distribution channels — no confirmed Senegal-specific distributor found, local representation would need establishing. For scenarios (b)-(d), a PowerChina/Sinohydro-style state EPC contractor is the more relevant reference class given their track record building complete plants regionally (see §4).

## 4. Chinese Solar Involvement — Precedents

- **Malicounda (22MW, Thiès) — 90% owned by CGN Europe Energy (China General Nuclear Power Corp's European renewables arm)**, commissioned Nov 2016, ~36GWh/yr, ~9,000 households. **The strongest direct, citable Chinese-Senegal solar precedent — anchor the proposal's credibility narrative on this.**
- Regional (proof of Chinese EPC capability in West/Central Africa): **Sinohydro/PowerChina** built the 20MWp Gardete plant (Guinea-Bissau) and studied the 20MW Lagdo plant (Cameroon); **Donsin plant (Burkina Faso)** financed via a €45M Chinese loan.
- Pattern: Chinese SOEs in SSA energy typically run an **"EPC + Finance" model** — Chinese contractor designs/builds under China Eximbank/Sinosure-backed loan, asset publicly/SPV-owned but Chinese-built.
- **Non-solar but relevant relationship precedent:** the **140MW Gouina Hydro plant** (Senegal/Mali/Mauritania) — three China Eximbank loans of ~$138.3M each (95% of ~$437M total cost), 20-yr maturity/9-yr grace/2% interest, completed 2022. Strong template for financing-structure language even though it's hydro.
- **Gap to flag honestly:** no large-scale Chinese-financed *utility-scale* Senegalese solar plant beyond the Malicounda equity stake was found — China's Senegal solar role to date is investor/owner (via CGN), not EPC contractor. Frame this project as "a logical next step deepening that relationship into an EPC/BOOT role," not as "continuing an existing EPC track record."

## 5. Financing / Incentive Angles

- **Sinosure**: articulated a "green development framework" for renewable-export underwriting; fully insured a 1GW PV project in Uzbekistan alongside Chinese banks. No Senegal-specific solar deal found, but directly applicable to de-risking a Chinese panel/EPC package here.
- **China Eximbank**: concessional Senegal power-lending precedent (Gouina hydro: 20yr/9yr grace/2%); has financed solar elsewhere in Africa (RMB 867.2M for the 54.66MW Garissa plant, Kenya) — a useful direct solar-financing comparable.
- **IFC/Scaling Solar/MIGA**: Kael+Kahone (44MWp) financed via IFC senior loans + Finland-IFC Blended Finance for Climate + EIB + Proparco, with **MIGA political-risk guarantees of €6.9M for up to 15 years** — a strong blended-finance template to structure around the PV component.
- **US DFC**: financed/insured the 158MW Taiba N'Diaye wind plant (~15% of Senegal's installed capacity) — shows DFC is an active Senegalese-renewables financier and potential co-financier/guarantor alongside Chinese capital (subject to current US-China DFI co-financing politics — check before including).
- **AfDB "Desert to Power"**: targets 10GW solar by 2030 across 11 Sahel countries incl. Senegal; the G5 Sahel Financing Facility has AfDB committing up to $379.6M + Green Climate Fund $150M, expected to leverage ~$437M more. Senegal is one of only 3 "notionally stable" countries among the 11 target states — more implementable location within this facility.
- **Senegal fiscal incentives**: solar/wind generation systems exempt from import duties and VAT; components get a preferential 5% import duty. General Investment Code adds equipment-import exemptions and (for qualifying investments) a reduced 15% corporate tax vs. standard rate — verify solar-specific applicability with local tax counsel.

## 6. Risks Specific to the PV Component

- **Import duties/customs**: solar/wind systems appear duty/VAT exempt, components at preferential 5% (general customs regime otherwise 0/5/10/20% under WAEMU/ECOWAS CET). **Battery (BESS) customs treatment not confirmed — batteries aren't always bundled into the same exemption category as PV modules elsewhere; open question for the financial model.**
- **Currency risk**: XOF is fixed to EUR (655.957:1) with an unconditional French Treasury guarantee. **A EUR-denominated/indexed PPA carries materially lower currency risk than a USD-denominated one** (XOF moves with EUR, not USD) — but Chinese EPC financing/equipment costs are typically USD-denominated, so residual EUR/USD cross-currency risk must be modeled. Recommend structuring the PV offtake in EUR or EUR-XOF-indexed terms rather than USD.
- **Technical risks**: dust/Harmattan soiling (see §2, the most material and well-documented risk); heat derating (needs Senegal-specific engineering study); grid curtailment/transmission bottlenecks (IEA-flagged, most relevant to scenarios (b)/(d) if exporting surplus — a self-contained microgrid avoids this but forgoes surplus-sale revenue, reinforcing scenario (c)'s case).
- **Land/rooftop rights**: ground-mount can use a **bail emphytéotique** (18-50yr, mortgageable) or **droit de superficie** (25-50yr, oriented to housing/building construction) — both require development within 24 months of grant. **Rooftop rights in a multi-unit complex have no confirmed Senegal-specific statute** — French/Moroccan/Quebec condominium-law analogs treat roofs as common area requiring co-owner-assembly approval, which if inherited into Senegalese practice means rooftop PV for individual units needs an HOA agreement or carved-out easement at point of sale. **This must be closed with Senegalese real-estate counsel before finalizing scenario (a)** — it also affects whether CRSE threshold calculations are per-unit or development-wide.

## Summary for the proposal team

1. **Lead the credibility narrative with CGN Europe Energy's 90% stake in Malicounda** — the strongest concrete Chinese-Senegal solar precedent.
2. **Regulatory sweet spot**: ≤1MW per site/building stays in the light "declaration" regime; anything larger needs the 25-year CRSE license — this should directly shape which scenario is pitched as "fastest to permit" (a) vs. "most attractive economics at scale" (b/c/d).
3. **Dust/Harmattan soiling is a genuinely underappreciated risk** (potential 25-50%+ seasonal loss without active cleaning) — budget it into every scenario's O&M line; an automated/robotic cleaning offer could be a Chinese-partner differentiator.
4. Several cost figures (rooftop/C&I $/kW premium, African BESS $/kWh) are **estimates, not sourced Senegal quotes** — firm up with actual EPC bids before they go into a financial model or investor-facing numbers.
5. Two legal gaps to close with counsel before finalizing: (a) exact PV threshold triggering CRSE license vs. declaration (verify against the primary Journal Officiel text of Decree 2023-286), and (b) rooftop/co-ownership rights in a multi-unit complex (no clear statutory answer found).
